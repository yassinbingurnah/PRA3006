What collagen is

  • Collagen is the main structural protein of connective tissue (skin, tendon, bone, cartilage, blood vessels).
  • Its structure is a triple helix of three α-chains, built from a repeating Gly–X–Y sequence where X and Y are often proline and hydroxyproline.
  • Key types:
    • Type I: skin, tendon, bone; mature scars
    • Type III: early wound tissue, blood vessels
    • Type IV: basement membranes
    • Type II: cartilage
  • It gives tissue tensile strength and acts as a scaffold that cells attach to and migrate along.

Collagen's role in each healing phase

  • Haemostasis (minutes): collagen exposed by injury and activates platelets (via GPVI, α2β1 and von Willebrand factor), which starts clotting.
  • Inflammation (days 1–4): collagen fragments attract neutrophils and macrophages.
  • Proliferation (days 4–21):
    • Macrophages release TGF-β and PDGF, which bring in fibroblasts.
    • Fibroblasts lay down mostly type III collagen in granulation tissue.
    • Myofibroblasts contract the wound.
  • Remodelling (weeks to over a year):
    • Type III is replaced by type I.
    • Fibres become cross-linked and line up along the lines of stress.
